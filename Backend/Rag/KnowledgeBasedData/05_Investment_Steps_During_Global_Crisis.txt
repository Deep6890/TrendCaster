FILE 05: INVESTMENT STEPS DURING GLOBAL CRISIS — WHEN SYSTEMS FAIL
================================================================
Context: Government instability, oil price crisis, currency collapse,
         banking stress, hyperinflation scenarios
Audience: Ordinary people — not institutional investors
================================================================

WHAT COUNTS AS A GLOBAL CRISIS (DEFINING THE SCENARIO)
================================================================
A global crisis for this file means conditions where:
  - Oil prices are extremely high or extremely volatile (>$100/barrel sustained)
  - Government fiscal systems are under severe stress (debt crisis, credit downgrade)
  - Inflation is running above 10% annually and possibly accelerating
  - Banking systems show signs of stress (bank runs, credit freezes)
  - Currency is weakening rapidly and unpredictably
  - Job markets are contracting significantly
  - Supply chains are disrupted (goods unavailable or extremely expensive)

Examples of such periods in recent history:
  - 2008 Global Financial Crisis (banking system failure, credit freeze)
  - 2020 COVID-19 shock (supply chain collapse, employment shock)
  - 2022 post-Ukraine war period (oil shock, food shock, inflation crisis globally)
  - 1997 Asian Financial Crisis (currency collapses across Southeast Asia)
  - Sri Lanka 2022 (government debt default, fuel shortage, economic collapse)

India has been partially shielded from the worst of these events historically due to:
  1. Large domestic consumption base (not heavily export-dependent)
  2. RBI's foreign exchange reserve management
  3. Capital account controls (foreign money cannot leave India overnight easily)

But India is NOT fully immune. A severe global oil crisis, a rupee freefall, or a 
domestic banking crisis would create real hardship for ordinary citizens.

================================================================
STEP 1: SECURE CASH LIQUIDITY FIRST — THE MOST IMPORTANT STEP
================================================================
In any crisis, the first thing that can fail is access to money. Bank ATMs run out 
of cash. Digital payments fail if server infrastructure is disrupted. Credit lines get 
frozen. In a severe crisis, the most valuable thing is immediate access to physical 
cash and basic goods.

Practical steps for cash security:
  - Maintain at least 1 month of living expenses in CASH at home (in a safe, hidden 
    location). This is not investment advice — this is survival preparedness. In the 
    Sri Lanka crisis of 2022, ATM queues stretched for hours and banks had withdrawal 
    limits. Physical cash on hand was critical.
  
  - Maintain 2-3 months of expenses in a savings or liquid fund that can be accessed 
    instantly online. Keep this in a separate bank from where your salary is deposited — 
    diversifying across two banks protects against a single bank having issues.

  - Do not keep excessive cash beyond 3 months of expenses at home — inflation erodes 
    its value rapidly during a crisis, and it's a security risk.

================================================================
STEP 2: REDUCE DISCRETIONARY DEBT IMMEDIATELY
================================================================
In a crisis, interest rates often spike. If you have variable rate debt (floating home 
loans, personal loans, credit card rollover balances), your EMI can increase 20-40% 
in a rate-spike scenario. The discipline of reducing debt before a crisis hits is 
what separates people who survive crises intact from those who don't.

Crisis debt priority hierarchy:
  1. Credit cards — pay to zero immediately and stop using for non-essentials
  2. Personal loans — accelerate repayment if you have surplus cash
  3. Vehicle loans — make at least one extra EMI payment per year to reduce principal
  4. Home loans — in a crisis, home loan restructuring options are often available 
     from banks. Know what your bank's forbearance policy is before you need it.

One key insight: In a crisis, DO NOT take out new loans to invest. The temptation to 
borrow and buy cheap assets (stocks, gold, property) is real during a crash, but it 
is dangerous. If the recovery takes longer than expected, you cannot service the debt 
and you are forced to sell assets at a loss.

================================================================
STEP 3: PROTECT FOOD AND ESSENTIAL GOODS — REAL ASSET DIVERSIFICATION
================================================================
This step sounds unusual in a financial planning document, but it is economically 
rational. During supply chain disruptions and hyperinflation, the value of food 
and essential goods appreciates dramatically in real terms.

Practical steps:
  - Maintain a 30-60 day stock of essential dry goods (rice, pulses, oil, salt, sugar, 
    flour) at home. This is not hoarding — it is prudent supply chain buffering.
    
  - A family of 4 spending ₹8,000/month on food staples could maintain a 2-month 
    stockpile worth ₹16,000. If food prices rise 25% during a crisis (as they did 
    in many countries in 2022), this stockpile effectively "earned" 25% in 2 months 
    on a real basis — far better than any investment.

  - This also provides psychological stability. When grocery shelves are empty or 
    prices are spiking, families with sufficient food reserves can make financial 
    decisions calmly rather than in panic.

================================================================
STEP 4: GOLD AS A CRISIS HEDGE — HOW MUCH AND WHAT FORM
================================================================
Gold has maintained purchasing power through every major economic crisis in modern 
history. During the 2008 crisis, gold rose 25%. During COVID-2020, gold hit all-time 
highs. When currencies collapse (as in Turkey, Argentina, Sri Lanka), people who held 
gold in those countries preserved wealth while those holding local currency savings 
lost 50-80% of purchasing power in dollar terms.

How much gold should a crisis-prepared person hold?
  - General guidance: 5% to 15% of total investable assets in gold
  - During a crisis, this allocation can rise to 20-25% as a defensive measure
  - Do not exceed 30-35% in gold — it does not produce income, and the opportunity 
    cost is high over long periods

What FORM of gold to hold during a crisis:
  Physical gold (coins and bars) — most direct. No counterparty risk. 
    In an extreme scenario where digital systems fail, physical gold is the only 
    form of gold that works. Recommend: Hallmark certified coins from a bank (SBI, 
    HDFC sell coins) or reputable jeweler. 22-24 karat coins in small denominations 
    (2g, 5g, 10g) are more liquid than one large 50g bar.

  Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs) — best if systems are functioning. 
    No risk of theft, earns 2.5% interest, zero long-term capital gains tax at 
    maturity. But they are digital — in a complete infrastructure failure, they are 
    inaccessible. For mild-to-moderate crises, SGBs are preferable. For extreme 
    scenarios, a mix of physical gold and SGBs is prudent.

  Gold ETFs — only if you have a demat account and markets are functional.

During a crisis, avoid gold jewelry purely as investment — making charges (10-25% 
of value) and purity uncertainty make it an inefficient gold holding.

================================================================
STEP 5: DIVERSIFY ACROSS CURRENCIES — PRACTICAL OPTIONS FOR INDIANS
================================================================
In an India-specific currency crisis (rupee falling sharply), having some exposure 
to foreign currencies protects wealth. This is not tax evasion — it is legitimate 
currency diversification within legal frameworks.

Legal ways for Indians to hold foreign currency exposure:
  - Foreign Currency Non-Resident (FCNR) Deposits: Fixed deposits in USD, GBP, or 
    EUR in Indian banks. Interest and principal are repaid in foreign currency. 
    Currently offering 4.5% to 6% on USD deposits (as of 2024). If the rupee weakens 
    further, you gain on both the interest and the exchange rate difference.

  - Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS): Indians can legally remit up to $250,000 
    per year abroad. This money can be used to invest in US index funds, international 
    stocks, or kept in a foreign bank. Requires Tax Collected at Source (TCS) of 20% 
    upfront (refundable when filing taxes). For significant portfolios (₹25 lakh+), 
    having 10-15% in international assets via LRS is a genuine currency hedge.

  - International Mutual Funds: Some Indian mutual funds invest in US or global equities. 
    However, SEBI currently has restrictions on new international fund flows (as of 
    early 2024) due to fund-of-fund rules. Check current availability when investing.

================================================================
STEP 6: DO NOT PANIC-SELL — THE MOST VIOLATED RULE IN EVERY CRISIS
================================================================
History documents this with remarkable consistency. In every financial crisis:
  - Markets fall dramatically (30-60% in severe cases)
  - People panic and sell near the bottom
  - Markets recover (in weeks for mild crises, months to years for severe ones)
  - The same people who sold at the bottom watch prices go past their original levels

The Nifty 50 index fell from 12,000 to 7,500 in the COVID crash of March 2020 
(a 37.5% drop). By December 2020, it had fully recovered. By 2024, it was above 
22,000. Someone who panic-sold in March 2020 locked in a 37% loss and then had to 
buy back at much higher prices to participate in the recovery.

What to do instead of panic-selling during a crisis:
  - Re-read your investment thesis. Have the fundamental reasons for your investment 
    changed, or only the price? Price falls are not evidence that an investment is bad.
  - If you have cash available, a crisis is the correct time to BUY more index funds 
    or quality assets — you are getting them at a discount.
  - If you genuinely cannot sleep because of market falls, your equity allocation is 
    too high for your psychological tolerance. Reduce equity permanently after the 
    crisis, not during it.

================================================================
STEP 7: REVIEW INCOME STABILITY — THE HUMAN CAPITAL HEDGE
================================================================
Your greatest financial asset during a crisis is usually not gold or stocks — it is 
your ability to earn income. Protecting and potentially enhancing your income during 
a crisis is the highest-return "investment" available to a working person.

Steps to protect income in a crisis:
  - Become indispensable in your current role: Volunteer for additional responsibilities, 
    develop skills that are hard to replace. Layoffs during downturns target the most 
    replaceable roles first.
  
  - Build multiple income streams before the crisis hits: Freelancing, tutoring, a 
    part-time skill-based business, rental income — any secondary income source that 
    can cushion a job loss. This is best built in good times; it is too late to start 
    when the crisis arrives.

  - Upskill into crisis-resistant domains: Healthcare, food production, government 
    services, essential IT infrastructure — these fields maintain employment even in 
    severe downturns. Luxury goods, construction, and real estate-related roles are 
    most vulnerable.
