FILE 09: INFLATION — COMPLETE HOUSEHOLD GUIDE
================================================================
What it is, how it's measured, how it hurts you, and how to fight it
================================================================

HOW INFLATION IS MEASURED IN INDIA
================================================================
India uses the Consumer Price Index (CPI) as its primary inflation benchmark. The 
CPI tracks the price of a "basket" of goods and services that a typical household 
buys. This basket is divided into categories with different weightings that reflect 
how much of a household's spending goes to each category.

The CPI basket composition in India (approximate weightings):
  Food and beverages: 45.9% (the largest component — India is food inflation sensitive)
  Housing: 10.1%
  Fuel and light: 6.8%
  Clothing and footwear: 6.5%
  Health: 5.9%
  Transport: 8.6%
  Education: 4.5%
  Other goods and services: 11.7%

This means India's inflation number is highly sensitive to food prices. A poor monsoon 
that raises vegetable prices by 30% can add nearly 2 percentage points to headline CPI 
even if everything else is stable. This "food inflation" is called supply-side inflation 
because it is caused by reduced supply, not by excess money in the economy.

Core inflation excludes food and fuel — it reflects the more persistent, demand-driven 
component of price rises. The RBI watches core inflation most closely when making 
rate decisions, because food inflation is temporary (next harvest brings prices down) 
while core inflation is stickier.

================================================================
TYPES OF INFLATION — WHY THE CAUSE MATTERS
================================================================
Demand-pull inflation: Too much money chasing too few goods. When the economy is 
growing fast, employment is high, and people are spending freely, sellers can raise 
prices because demand is strong. The cure is raising interest rates (which reduces 
spending) or reducing government stimulus spending. India's post-COVID recovery 
period (2021-2022) had elements of demand-pull inflation.

Cost-push inflation: Input costs rise, forcing producers to raise prices. The classic 
example is oil price rises — when fuel costs more, every product that requires 
transport becomes more expensive. This is what India experienced in 2022 when Russia's 
invasion of Ukraine pushed oil and food commodity prices up. The RBI raising rates 
does NOT fix cost-push inflation — it only increases the pain without addressing the 
cause. But the RBI often raises rates anyway to control inflation expectations.

Built-in inflation (wage-price spiral): Workers demand higher wages due to higher 
prices. Companies pay higher wages and then raise prices to cover costs. This creates 
a self-reinforcing loop. This is what happened in the UK and US in the 1970s and 
what central banks fear most today.

Imported inflation: India imports oil, edible oils, fertilizers, electronics — all 
priced in US dollars. When global commodity prices rise OR when the rupee weakens 
(making dollar-denominated imports costlier), inflation enters India's economy 
through imports. A 10% weakening of the rupee alone can add 0.5-1% to India's CPI.

SECTOR-BY-SECTOR IMPACT OF INFLATION ON HOUSEHOLD LIFE
================================================================
FOOD: Most directly and immediately felt. Vegetable prices in India can rise 50-100% 
in weeks after a bad harvest. Unlike manufactured goods, food prices are volatile — 
they can fall just as fast when supply normalises. The pain is real but often temporary.

FUEL: Petrol and diesel prices in India are now partially market-linked. Global crude 
oil price rises are eventually passed through to consumers — with a government buffer 
that delayed some 2022 price hikes. Higher fuel prices affect everything that moves 
(food transport, daily commute, raw material delivery).

EMIs: Rising interest rates raise your floating rate EMI. A 2% repo rate hike cycle 
(which India went through in 2022-2023) on a ₹30 lakh home loan meant EMIs rose 
by ₹3,500-4,000/month. For a family earning ₹60,000/month, this is 6-7% of income 
suddenly absorbed by a higher loan cost.

EDUCATION: School and college fees, coaching institute fees, and stationery costs 
tend to rise 8-12% annually in India — faster than headline CPI. This makes 
education a significant inflation source for middle-class families.

HEALTHCARE: Medical costs — hospital charges, medicines, diagnostic tests — are 
rising 8-15% annually. Without health insurance, a hospitalization can consume 
years of savings. Healthcare inflation is one of the most damaging but most 
under-protected areas for Indian families.

HOW TO FIGHT INFLATION PERSONALLY
================================================================
The single most powerful individual action against inflation is NOT a specific 
investment — it is increasing your income at a rate that exceeds inflation. If 
inflation is 6% and your salary rises 8% per year, you are staying ahead. If 
your salary is stagnant while prices rise, you are falling behind regardless of 
what investments you hold.

Beyond income, several financial tools help specifically against inflation:

Gold as inflation hedge: Gold has historically maintained purchasing power over 
very long periods. Over 20-year horizons, gold returns roughly track inflation 
plus a small real return. It is not a wealth-builder — it is a wealth-preserver.

Equity as inflation fighter: Well-run companies pass input cost increases to 
consumers (pricing power), meaning corporate profits — and therefore stock prices — 
often outpace inflation over time. Over any 15-year period in Indian market history, 
equity returns have significantly exceeded inflation. This is why even conservative 
investors should have some equity in their long-term portfolio.

Real assets: Property, farmland, and other physical assets tend to appreciate with 
inflation over time because the cost of building or producing them rises.

Index-linked instruments: Some government bonds in India (Inflation Indexed Bonds 
or IIBs) are indexed to CPI — their interest payment rises with inflation. However, 
their availability is currently limited for retail investors.

Avoid excessive cash holding: Every rupee sitting in a low-interest savings account 
is being eroded by inflation every single day. The invisible cost of holding excess 
cash is the single biggest wealth destroyer for ordinary people.

================================================================
INFLATION WATCH — WHAT TO MONITOR REGULARLY
================================================================
Monthly CPI release (by MoSPI, usually on the 12th-14th of each month): The headline 
CPI number gives you the overall picture. Focus on food CPI specifically, as this 
most directly reflects your grocery bill.

WPI (Wholesale Price Index): Released monthly. Measures price changes at the 
wholesale level, before goods reach consumers. A rising WPI often predicts rising 
CPI with a lag of 1-3 months — it is an early warning indicator.

Kharif and Rabi crop outlooks: India's agricultural ministry provides crop production 
estimates twice a year. Poor kharif (summer) crop estimates in August-September 
typically predict rising vegetable and cereal prices in October-December.

RBI Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) statements: Released 6 times per year. The RBI 
explicitly states its inflation target (4%, with a 2-6% tolerance band) and whether 
current inflation is above or below target. This directly signals whether rate hikes 
or cuts are coming — which affects your EMIs and savings rates.
