Covering: The transmission mechanism from global oil prices to your grocery bill,
          EMI, job, and daily expenses

THE OIL PRICE TRANSMISSION MECHANISM
================================================================
When global crude oil prices rise, the impact does not stop at your petrol pump. 
It travels through the entire economy in a chain reaction that affects you in 
ways you may not immediately associate with oil. Understanding this chain helps 
you anticipate problems and prepare in advance rather than react in panic.

Stage 1 — Crude oil price rises globally. This can happen due to OPEC production 
cuts, geopolitical conflict in oil-producing regions, or a sudden surge in global 
demand. In 2022, the Russia-Ukraine war pushed Brent crude from $75 to $130/barrel.

Stage 2 — Petrol and diesel prices at Indian pumps rise. India imports 85% of crude 
oil requirements. The government partially shields consumers through taxes and subsidies, 
but large sustained oil price rises inevitably pass through. In 2022, Indian petrol 
prices rose ₹10-12 per litre in a government-deferred but unavoidable adjustment.

Stage 3 — Transport costs rise across the economy. Trucks, buses, trains, ships, 
and flights all run on fuel. When diesel prices rise 10%, freight costs for every 
goods movement in India rise proportionally. This cost is embedded in every product 
that moves on roads — vegetables, grain, consumer electronics, furniture, clothes.

Stage 4 — Food prices rise. Agricultural produce that arrives from farms to cities 
carries the higher transport cost. Additionally, agricultural inputs like fertilizers 
(which are made from natural gas, priced similarly to oil) also rise in cost. Farmers 
face higher production costs and higher transport costs simultaneously.

Stage 5 — Manufacturing input costs rise. Plastic is made from petrochemicals. 
Paints, chemicals, textiles, rubber — a huge range of manufactured goods uses 
petroleum derivatives. When crude rises, all these input costs rise, pushing up 
the price of everyday manufactured goods.

Stage 6 — Services become more expensive. Your auto-rickshaw driver, local delivery 
person, and plumber all pay more for fuel. They pass this to you in higher service 
charges — sometimes visibly, sometimes by reducing the value delivered.

Stage 7 — RBI responds by raising interest rates. Since oil-driven inflation shows 
up in CPI numbers, the RBI often raises the repo rate to signal inflation control, 
even though rate hikes cannot fix supply-driven oil inflation. This raises your 
home loan EMI and reduces disposable income further.

Stage 8 — Government revenues are stressed. A weaker rupee (caused by higher import 
costs) and higher import bill narrows fiscal space. The government may cut subsidies 
on cooking gas, fertilizers, or food grains — which further adds to household costs.

SPECIFIC HOUSEHOLD IMPACT QUANTIFICATION
================================================================
The following calculations are based on a median Indian urban household 
(monthly income ₹50,000) and a hypothetical 30% crude oil price rise 
(from $80 to $104/barrel):

Petrol price increase: Approximately ₹6-8/litre. For a household with one 
two-wheeler consuming 60 litres/month: +₹420/month increase.

Auto-rickshaw and cab fares: Typically rise 8-12% after a sustained oil price 
rise. For a daily commuter spending ₹1,500/month on auto: +₹135-180/month.

Grocery and vegetables: Transport cost increase adds approximately 5-8% to 
fresh produce prices over 2-3 months. On a ₹6,000/month grocery bill: 
+₹300-480/month.

LPG cooking gas: If the government adjusts the heavily subsidized LPG price, 
an increase of ₹50-150/cylinder is typical. At 1.5 cylinders/month: 
+₹75-225/month.

Total additional monthly burden on one household: Approximately ₹930 to 
₹1,305/month — roughly 1.9% to 2.6% of a ₹50,000 monthly income. This may 
seem small in percentage terms, but for households at the margin (spending 90%+ 
of income on necessities), this is the difference between a balanced monthly 
budget and accumulating debt.

SECTORS THAT SUFFER MOST DURING AN OIL CRISIS
================================================================
Aviation: Fuel is 30-40% of airline operating costs. When oil spikes, airlines 
raise ticket prices rapidly and reduce unprofitable routes. Domestic travel becomes 
less affordable, affecting tourism-dependent businesses and hospitality workers.

Trucking and logistics: Directly impacted. Small truck operators with thin margins 
face business viability questions. This contributes to supply chain stress — fewer 
trucks running means delivery delays and goods shortages.

Petrochemical industries: Plastics, paints, packaging materials, synthetic fibres, 
fertilizers — any industry with petrochemical inputs faces margin pressure. Companies 
either absorb the cost (reduce profits, potentially affecting employment) or pass it 
on (raise consumer prices).

Small and medium businesses: SMEs have less ability to absorb cost increases or pass 
them on quickly compared to large companies. An oil price crisis that squeezes SME 
margins often leads to reduced hiring, wage freezes, and in extreme cases, closures. 
The employment effect on households comes through this channel.

================================================================
SECTORS THAT BENEFIT FROM HIGH OIL PRICES
================================================================
Oil exploration and production companies: ONGC, Oil India — their revenues rise 
directly with oil prices. Upstream oil stocks tend to perform well during oil price 
spikes. However, the government often imposes windfall taxes on these companies 
during extreme price rises, partially capping their gains.

Coal and alternative energy: High oil prices make coal-fired and nuclear power 
relatively cheaper and increase the economic attractiveness of solar, wind, and 
electric vehicles. India's renewable energy sector and EV manufacturers can benefit 
from sustained high oil prices as the economic case for switching strengthens.

Fertilizer companies (with caveats): Fertilizer input costs rise with oil/gas prices, 
but the government typically increases fertilizer subsidies to protect farmers during 
oil crises. Some fertilizer companies may benefit from higher subsidy payments.

================================================================
WHAT YOU SHOULD DO IF AN OIL PRICE CRISIS APPEARS IMMINENT
================================================================
Monitoring signals that suggest an imminent oil price surge: OPEC+ announces 
production cuts greater than 1 million barrels/day; major conflict erupts in the 
Persian Gulf or involves major oil producers; US-Iran or Russia-West tensions 
escalate toward confrontation.

Practical steps 1-3 months before prices peak:
  Fill up vehicle fuel tanks when prices are still lower — this is not speculation, 
  it is household supply management.
  
  Stock up on shelf-stable food items that use significant packaging (cooking oil, 
  processed foods, packaged goods) before prices increase. Store appropriately.
  
  If you were considering a long-distance air trip, book tickets before oil price 
  rises fully translate into higher airfares (typically a lag of 1-3 months).
  
  If you have a floating rate home loan and are near a rate decision cycle, consider 
  whether switching to a fixed rate makes sense before rate hikes arrive.

These are rational economic responses to a foreseeable price shock — not 
panic-driven hoarding.
